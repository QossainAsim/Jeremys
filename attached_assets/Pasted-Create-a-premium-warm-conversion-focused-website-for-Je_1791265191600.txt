Create a premium, warm, conversion-focused website for **Jeremys Bread Kitchen**, an organic artisan bakery in Åbyhøj, Aarhus, Denmark.

The goal is NOT to make the bakery look like a large commercial chain. The website should preserve the feeling of a small, authentic, highly personal bakery where bread is made carefully in small batches.

The primary business goal is to turn people who discover Jeremys through Facebook, Instagram, Google, or word-of-mouth into customers who want to **see what's available, reserve/order products, and visit the bakery**.

## BRAND FEEL

The design should feel:

- Handmade
- Warm
- Authentic
- Premium but not luxurious
- Organic
- Local
- Scandinavian
- Craft-focused
- Personal
- Appetite-inducing

Avoid:
- Corporate design
- Generic bakery templates
- Excessive animations
- Stock photography
- Fake luxury branding
- Overly complicated navigation
- “AI” language
- Anything that makes the bakery look mass-produced

Use the real bakery photographs provided by the client wherever possible. The bread and pastries should be the visual focus.

## HOMEPAGE STRUCTURE

### 1. HERO SECTION

Use a large, beautiful real photograph of Jeremys bread as the hero background/image.

Headline:

**Organic bread, baked with care in Åbyhøj.**

Supporting text:

**Sourdough bread, pastries, cakes and more — handmade in small batches with carefully selected ingredients.**

Primary CTA:

**ORDER / PRE-ORDER**

Secondary CTA:

**SEE THIS WEEK'S BAKES**

Make the primary CTA visually obvious.

The first screen should immediately communicate:

1. What they sell
2. Why it is special
3. Where they are
4. What the visitor can do next

Do not put a large paragraph above the fold.

---

### 2. THIS WEEK AT JEREMYS

Create a highly visual product section immediately below the hero.

Heading:

**Fresh from the bakery**

Show products in attractive cards with large photographs.

Categories:

- Bread
- Sourdough
- Pastries
- Cakes
- Buns
- Pizza dough

Each product card should contain:

- Photograph
- Product name
- Short appetizing description
- Price
- Availability
- Order / Reserve button

Design this section so that the visitor immediately thinks:

**“That looks good. I want one.”**

Use real product photography rather than generic stock images.

---

### 3. LIMITED BATCH / PRE-ORDER MESSAGE

Create a visually distinctive section communicating the small-batch nature of the bakery.

Headline:

**Small batches. When they're gone, they're gone.**

Supporting text:

**Our bread and pastries are made in limited quantities. Reserve yours ahead of time so you don't miss out.**

CTA:

**RESERVE YOUR BAKE**

This should create natural scarcity without sounding manipulative.

---

### 4. WHY JEREMYS

Create a short storytelling section.

Headline:

**Bread made the way we believe it should be.**

Explain the bakery's philosophy around:

- Organic ingredients
- Long fermentation
- Handmade production
- Small batches
- Traditional craftsmanship
- Quality over quantity

Keep the copy short and human.

Use a photograph of Jeremy, the bakery, or the production process if available.

Do not make this section sound corporate.

---

### 5. PRODUCT PHOTOGRAPHY / VISUAL STORY

Create a full-width visual section using the provided bakery photographs.

Show:

- Fresh bread
- Pastries
- Cakes
- Dough
- Bakery shelves
- Production

Use minimal text.

The objective is to make the visitor hungry.

---

### 6. CUSTOMER LOVE

Create a social-proof section.

Heading:

**Loved by our customers**

Display 3–5 short customer testimonials from genuine public reviews once verified.

Include the overall review rating if it is verified.

Do not fabricate testimonials.

Add:

**See more reviews**

linking to the appropriate review platform.

The design should make the bakery feel trusted and established within the local community.

---

### 7. FACEBOOK / COMMUNITY

Jeremy's Facebook page is an important part of the business and already has an active audience.

Create a section called:

**Fresh from Jeremys**

Show recent Facebook/Instagram-style posts or a visual social feed if technically possible.

Supporting text:

**Follow us for fresh bakes, what's coming out of the oven, and what's available this week.**

CTA:

**FOLLOW JEREMYS**

The website should complement Facebook rather than replace it.

The desired customer journey is:

**Facebook post → Website → Product → Order/Reserve → Pickup**

---

### 8. ORDER / PRE-ORDER

Create a dedicated ordering experience.

The visitor should be able to:

1. Choose a product
2. Select quantity
3. Select available pickup date
4. Provide name/contact information
5. Confirm the order
6. Receive confirmation

Keep the process extremely simple.

If a full payment/order system cannot be implemented yet, create the complete UI/UX and structure so it can later connect to an appropriate ordering/payment system.

The ordering system must support the reality of a small bakery where product availability can change and quantities can be limited.

## NAVIGATION

Keep navigation minimal:

**Home**
**Order**
**This Week**
**Our Story**
**Visit**
**Contact**

Always keep a prominent:

**ORDER / PRE-ORDER**

button in the navigation.

On mobile, make the ordering CTA especially easy to reach.

---

## MOBILE FIRST

A large percentage of visitors may discover Jeremys through Facebook or Instagram on their phones.

The mobile experience therefore needs to be excellent.

Prioritize:

- Fast loading
- Large product images
- Large buttons
- Simple navigation
- Easy ordering
- Click-to-call
- Click-to-message
- Map/directions

A visitor should be able to go from a Facebook post to placing an order with as little friction as possible.

---

## CONVERSION PSYCHOLOGY

Design the website around this sequence:

**DISCOVER → DESIRE → TRUST → AVAILABILITY → ACTION**

The visitor should first see beautiful food.

Then understand why it is special.

Then see that other customers love it.

Then understand what is available.

Then have an obvious way to order/reserve.

Do not overwhelm the visitor with information before showing the products.

The website should sell the experience of getting freshly made artisan bread—not simply provide information about a bakery.

---

## IMPORTANT BUSINESS PRINCIPLE

Do not position Jeremys as a mass-market bakery.

The opportunity is to help the bakery **sell more without losing the craftsmanship and personal character that makes people love it.**

The website should make the business feel like:

**“A special local bakery that you want to discover and come back to.”**

not:

**“A large bakery chain.”**

---

## FUTURE EXPANSION

Structure the website so it can eventually support:

- Online pre-orders
- Scheduled pickup
- Special cake orders
- Catering/event orders
- Wholesale inquiries
- Local delivery
- Customer accounts
- Product availability management
- Email/newsletter
- Gift cards
- Potential expansion beyond Aarhus

Do NOT imply that Jeremys currently offers these services unless confirmed.

Build the architecture so they can be added later.

---

## VISUAL DIRECTION

Use:

- Warm natural photography
- Large food photography
- Generous white/cream space
- Subtle Scandinavian typography
- Organic shapes where appropriate
- Minimal animations
- Clear buttons
- Elegant but approachable typography

The photographs should do most of the selling.

The website should feel like you can almost smell the bread through the screen.

## FINAL QUALITY TEST

Before considering the website finished, ask:

“If someone discovers Jeremys through a Facebook post at 8 AM, can they understand what Jeremys sells, see something they want, know where to get it, and reserve/order it within 30–60 seconds?”

If not, simplify the experience.

The website should ultimately save the bakery owner time—not create another complicated system for them to manage.
